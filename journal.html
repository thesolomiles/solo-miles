2026-08-22 · Friday

  • Booted the r3f dev server to review state. Spotted a big dark slab lying on the ground near the town centre.
  • Root-caused it: not in runtime code and not a distinct object — it was terrain faces in Ground UV-mapped to grey swatches (albedo #96–#ab), which read near-black under the moody lighting. Two grey regions: a centreline strip (x≈0, running north from town centre, 135 faces) and the raised north-hill strip (254 faces).
  • Recognised them as the intended dirt road / trail out of town (cf. fe0964 "texture the dirt road") that had been dropped on the wrong swatch. Remapped all 390 grey faces onto the dirt swatch (0.459, 0.283) the riverbanks already use — pure imphenzia collapse-onto-swatch. Ground now has just two UV cells: grass (6385) + dirt (824).
  • Re-exported public/models/town.glb (Draco, ~188 KB; all 15 nodes + 5 materials incl. Water and the emissive Material.004 preserved). Verified in-browser: the slab is gone, replaced by a warm dirt road running north between house and café to the river.
  • Housekeeping: Blender had been left in Edit Mode on Ground from last session — tabbed back to Object Mode (non-destructive).
  • Noted for later: TownModel.tsx still keys houseGlow on a HouseWindowsLit material that no longer exists (unified onto Material.004 on 2026-08-21), so the house-glow knob is now a no-op.
  • Brightened the scene: tuned the live ?debug lighting panel and baked the result into LIGHTING_DEFAULTS (lighting.ts) — Sun 2.35→4, Sky fill 0.32→0.84, Cool fill 0.2→1.5, Bloom 0.5→0.9, Bloom cutoff 0.8→0.53, Contrast 0.18→0.12, Vignette 0.75→0.09, Occlusion 2.3→0.75. Verified via the clean (non-debug) URL: production now renders bright and vibrant instead of moody-dim.
  • Retaining wall → grey concrete: the steep north terrain banks (the "retaining wall") were brown dirt tiles; remapped all 255 wall faces (steep + a few cap/footing) onto the grey swatch (0.399,0.976) so it reads as concrete.
  • North forest: scattered a thick low-poly pine forest across the two highlighted north regions (either side of the trail) — 159 linked-mesh duplicates of the 4 pine variants, organic scale/yaw/lean, Poisson-ish spacing that tapers near the river bank (sparse at the water, impenetrable at the back). Named PineTree_* (no underscore) to match existing pines — i.e. NOT auto-densified/collided by the Pine_ web systems.
  • Road → tarmac: settled the road question — it's asphalt now. Recolored the 135 flat centerline road faces onto a dark neutral swatch #3a3a3a at UV (0.445,0.949) (verified non-emissive first, per the swatch-glow gotcha).
  • Built a bridge (new Bridge object, 258 faces, on Material.004): tarmac deck spanning the river gap (world Y 5.5–15.3, flush with the road at Z0), with white parapet railings down both sides (posts + top/kick rails + balusters, white swatch (0.031,0.469)). Connects the town road to the forest road across the water.
  • Re-exported town.glb (Draco, ~233 KB, 176 mesh objects). Verified in the web app: tarmac road, bridge (walked onto the deck — parapets + water read correctly), and the dense forest all render.
  • Found while verifying: the player walked clean off the north edge into white void. Collision (incl. the square roam clamp) is still disabled in Player.tsx, so nothing bounds the player. Note for when it's re-enabled: WORLD.boundary is 54 but the actual Ground is only ~±28, so the clamp needs to match the real terrain half-extent.
  • Fixed "character looks huge": root cause was the buildings, not the player. The player is ~1.8u (character.glb ×0.9); the house had shrunk to 3.48u so the character read as half its height. Traced it to the map-scale bake — the buildings' size relied on TownModel scale=2, and flipping that 2→1 (to fix the giant terrain) also halved the buildings (6.97→3.48u). The terrain was authored true-scale so it was unaffected. Fix: scaled the house + café groups 2× in Blender about their base footprints — house back to 6.97u, café 6.57u (exactly the journal's original targets, ~3.9× the player), still seated at Z0 and clear of road/river. Player/camera/speed untouched. Re-exported and verified in-game: player now reads correctly small beside a proper 2-story house.
  • Fixed the "weird lines" on the water. Root cause: the Blender Water material exports as glTF KHR_materials_transmission + IOR 1.33 + roughness 0.04 (glass), and three.js renders that as a MeshPhysicalMaterial doing screen-space refraction — on a faceted, double-sided, shadow-receiving low-poly mesh that streaks with diagonal artifacts (the "water = a three.js job on export" the art pass always intended). Fix (code-only, TownModel.tsx): detect the mesh whose material is named Water and swap in a stylized MeshStandardMaterial (flat-shaded, DoubleSide, translucent blue, receiveShadow=false) — no refraction pass, no acne. Gotcha: first tried FrontSide and the water went black (the mesh's faces are wound downward, which is why it exported doubleSided) → must use DoubleSide.
  • Chasing a black void at the bridge: proved via a red-material test that it's NOT the water — it's the tarmac (road + bridge deck, swatch #3a3a3a) rendering near-black because 0.22 reflectance gets crushed by the scene lighting. Leonard adjusted the tarmac color himself in Blender (hands-off). NOTE: that Blender edit won't reach the web until town.glb is re-exported — the shipped glb still has the old tarmac.
  • Rocks + vegetation pass on the river (from a real-river reference). Built 3 low-poly boulder meshes (flattened jittered icospheres, rock swatch #babbc0) and 3 bush meshes (clustered green blobs, grass swatch) and scattered, seated on the terrain, across the river corridor by zone (classified Ground faces into wet bed Z<−0.55 / dirt bank / grass fringe): 155 rocks (100 in-water poking through the surface + 55 bigger on the banks), 130 grass tufts (dup'd GrassScrub, size-varied so big ones read as scrub), 34 bushes. Bridge corridor (x −2.5..3.5) kept clear; all decorative (names avoid the Pine_/Rock_ collider prefixes). Re-exported town.glb (Draco, ~325 KB, now 498 meshes) and verified in-game — reads like the rocky reference. This export also baked in Leonard's own tarmac-color edit (road now renders as grey, not the near-black void).
  • River refinement pass (Leonard's notes): reduced rocks 155→45, deleted all bank rocks so rocks live only in the water (banks keep grass + bushes), and matched the bridge deck tarmac to the road. Leonard's road-tarmac edit had moved the swatch to (0.416, 0.958); the bridge deck top sat 0.01 off at (0.416, 0.968) (a darker swatch) — remapped the deck faces to the road's exact swatch. Side effect: the deck's grey concrete side faces (0.399) were within tolerance and also got the tarmac swatch, so the whole deck is now uniform tarmac (fine/cleaner). Down to 388 objects, ~296 KB.
  • Watch: the town.glb is now 388 objects (rocks/grass/bushes/pines are individual linked-mesh duplicates). Frustum culling covers it for now, but if draw-call perf bites, merge each scatter group into one mesh or use instancing.
  • Still open (unchanged): BUILDINGS.pos in town.ts (±22) doesn't match the glb building positions (±7.9) → labels/interact zones float over wrong ground; collision still disabled in Player.tsx (now also needs the bigger building footprints when re-enabled).

2026-08-21 · Thursday

  • Diagnosed why the house window read brighter than the cafe's: the house used a one-off HouseWindowsLit material (hand-tuned orange, emission ×1.6) instead of the shared Imphenzia palette material.
  • Unified the house window onto the shared Material.004 — reassigned the window faces, collapsed their UVs onto the lit-window swatch (0.508, 0.492), deleted the orphan material.
  • Learned the real driver of the brightness gap: emissive brightness scales with face area. The house's window faces are ~324× the cafe's emissive area in world space, so the shared white ×5 emission blew out.
  • Matched the house window color to the cafe's (was pure white).
  • Created the imphenzia-assets skill (.claude/skills/) to standardize the single-palette method for all future assets.
  • Started this dev journal.
  • Experiment: created a low-poly pine tree in a new Landscape collection via the imphenzia-assets method (shared Material.004, UVs collapsed onto trunk + 3 pine-green swatches). Skill validated. Tree is Blender-only, not yet exported to town.glb.
  • Building scale saga (context recovered from a prior session): buildings were ~6× player → halved → +30%; final house 6.97u (~3.9×), café 6.57u (~3.7×). Confirmed against current Blender scales (house 1.432, café 0.624).
  • Known bug (still open): BUILDINGS.pos in town.ts no longer matches where the rebuilt buildings sit — labels/interact zones float over wrong ground.
  • Scale lives in TWO places that compound: Blender per-object scale × web scale=2 in TownModel.tsx. Goal: model at true 1:1 size, apply scales in Blender, set web scale to 1, so nothing is ever scaled at runtime.
  • Removed all building interactions (kept forest trail) and temporarily disabled collision (commented out resolveCollisions in Player.tsx) while the town is re-laid-out.
  • Fresh start on the landscape: deleted the old river + all its leftovers (Water, RiverRocks, orphaned materials) down to house/café/tree/ground.
  • Rebuilt the river the Imphenzia way, with a real dug channel: replaced the flat 19-poly ground with a low-poly grid terrain and carved a meandering trench into it (grass top + dirt banks, all on Material.004). Organic meander, varying width, steeper banks, scattered rocks.
  • Water is now its own faceted low-poly mesh with a dedicated translucent + reflective Water material (the one sanctioned exception to the single-palette rule) — you see the dug bed through it, so depth is real geometry, not painted color. Final reflective/translucent shading will be a three.js job on export.
  • Gotcha caught: picked a water swatch by color alone and it turned out to be an emissive swatch → the water blew out white. Fixed by remapping to the matte blue of the same color. Both lessons folded into the imphenzia-assets skill.
  • Landscape: raised the north strip on a smoothstep gradient (gentle hill rising toward the north), added a low-poly grass scrub, and scattered 3 more organic pines (varied tier count, size, lean, vertex jitter, green mix) so they don't read as clones.
  • Exported the whole scene to public/models/town.glb (Draco, 155 KB) — terrain, river+water, rocks, 4 pines, grass, house, café.
  • Baked out the runtime scale: the new landscape is authored at true size (~56u), so the old TownModel scale=2 made everything giant. Flipped it to scale=1 — the town now renders at 1:1 with the player correctly proportioned. Going forward, assets are modelled at true world size and nothing is scaled at runtime.
