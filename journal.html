2026-08-21

2026-08-21 · Thursday

  • Diagnosed why the house window read brighter than the cafe's: the house used a one-off HouseWindowsLit material (hand-tuned orange, emission ×1.6) instead of the shared Imphenzia palette material.
  • Unified the house window onto the shared Material.004 — reassigned the window faces, collapsed their UVs onto the lit-window swatch (0.508, 0.492), deleted the orphan material.
  • Learned the real driver of the brightness gap: emissive brightness scales with face area. The house's window faces are ~324× the cafe's emissive area in world space, so the shared white ×5 emission blew out.
  • Matched the house window color to the cafe's (was pure white).
  • Created the imphenzia-assets skill (.claude/skills/) to standardize the single-palette method for all future assets.
  • Started this dev journal.
  • Experiment: created a low-poly pine tree in a new Landscape collection via the imphenzia-assets method (shared Material.004, UVs collapsed onto trunk + 3 pine-green swatches). Skill validated. Tree is Blender-only, not yet exported to town.glb.
  • Building scale saga (context recovered from a prior session): buildings were ~6× player → halved → +30%; final house 6.97u (~3.9×), café 6.57u (~3.7×). Confirmed against current Blender scales (house 1.432, café 0.624).
  • Known bug (still open): BUILDINGS.pos in town.ts no longer matches where the rebuilt buildings sit — labels/interact zones float over wrong ground.
  • Scale lives in TWO places that compound: Blender per-object scale × web scale=2 in TownModel.tsx. Goal: model at true 1:1 size, apply scales in Blender, set web scale to 1, so nothing is ever scaled at runtime.
  • Removed all building interactions (kept forest trail) and temporarily disabled collision (commented out resolveCollisions in Player.tsx) while the town is re-laid-out.
  • Fresh start on the landscape: deleted the old river + all its leftovers (Water, RiverRocks, orphaned materials) down to house/café/tree/ground.
  • Rebuilt the river the Imphenzia way, with a real dug channel: replaced the flat 19-poly ground with a low-poly grid terrain and carved a meandering trench into it (grass top + dirt banks, all on Material.004). Organic meander, varying width, steeper banks, scattered rocks.
  • Water is now its own faceted low-poly mesh with a dedicated translucent + reflective Water material (the one sanctioned exception to the single-palette rule) — you see the dug bed through it, so depth is real geometry, not painted color. Final reflective/translucent shading will be a three.js job on export.
  • Gotcha caught: picked a water swatch by color alone and it turned out to be an emissive swatch → the water blew out white. Fixed by remapping to the matte blue of the same color. Both lessons folded into the imphenzia-assets skill.
  • Landscape: raised the north strip on a smoothstep gradient (gentle hill rising toward the north), added a low-poly grass scrub, and scattered 3 more organic pines (varied tier count, size, lean, vertex jitter, green mix) so they don't read as clones.
  • Exported the whole scene to public/models/town.glb (Draco, 155 KB) — terrain, river+water, rocks, 4 pines, grass, house, café.
  • Baked out the runtime scale: the new landscape is authored at true size (~56u), so the old TownModel scale=2 made everything giant. Flipped it to scale=1 — the town now renders at 1:1 with the player correctly proportioned. Going forward, assets are modelled at true world size and nothing is scaled at runtime.
